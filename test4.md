### Section F: Define the Following Terms

**1.** **Eclipse:** An eclipse is an astronomical event that takes place when one heavenly body, such as the Moon or a planet, moves into the shadow of another heavenly body.
**2.** **Solar eclipse:** It occurs when the Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth and blocks the light of the Sun from reaching the Earth.
**3.** **Lunar eclipse:** It occurs on a full moon night when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, and only the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.
**4.** **Umbra:** It is the dark central part of the shadow cast by the Moon on the Earth.
**5.** **Penumbra:** It is the outer shadow that gets bigger while reaching the Earth.
**6.** **Total solar eclipse:** It occurs when the umbra covers a small part of the Earth and the Moon completely covers the Sun. It can be seen only from the exact location.
**7.** **Partial solar eclipse:** It is the eclipse seen by people in the penumbra location, where the Moon covers only a part of the Sun.
**8.** **Total lunar eclipse:** It occurs when the Moon and the Sun are on exact opposite sides of the Earth and the Moon appears red.
**9.** **Partial lunar eclipse:** It occurs when the Earth's shadow covers only a section of the Moon. One section looks completely dark while the rest remains illuminated.
**10.** **Orbiting:** It means to move along a curved path.
**11.** **Celestial bodies:** They are heavenly bodies, such as the Sun, Moon, Earth and planets, that are part of the universe.

### Section G: Short Questions with Answers (2 Marks Each)

**1.** What is an eclipse?

- **Answer:** An eclipse takes place when one heavenly body, such as the Moon or a planet, moves into the shadow of another heavenly body.
  **2.** Name the two kinds of eclipses.
- **Answer:** The two kinds of eclipses are solar eclipse and lunar eclipse.
  **3.** When does a solar eclipse occur?
- **Answer:** A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth. The Moon blocks the Sun's light from reaching the Earth.
  **4.** What lasts for a maximum of 7 minutes 30 seconds?
- **Answer:** A total solar eclipse lasts for a maximum of 7 minutes 30 seconds.
  **5.** Why do the Moon and the Sun both appear to be of the same size?
- **Answer:** The Sun is about 400 times larger than the Moon, but it is also about 400 times farther from the Earth. This size-distance match makes them look equal in size.
  **6.** Why does the Moon appear red during a total lunar eclipse?
- **Answer:** Some sunlight passes through the Earth's atmosphere and falls on the Moon. The blue light gets filtered out, so the Moon appears red.
  **7.** On which day of the month is a lunar eclipse most likely to occur?
- **Answer:** A lunar eclipse is most likely to occur on a full moon day or night.
  **8.** How should you watch a solar eclipse?
- **Answer:** Watch it only with proper eclipse glasses or another safe viewing method. Never look directly at the Sun.
  **9.** Why is it harmful to look at a solar eclipse directly?
- **Answer:** A solar eclipse can harm your eyes. Neither ordinary sunglasses nor coloured glasses are safe.
  **10.** Why is a lunar eclipse safe to watch?
- **Answer:** A lunar eclipse lasts for a few hours and there is no harm in looking at it.
  **11.** Differentiate between umbra and penumbra.
- **Answer:** The umbra is the dark central shadow, and the penumbra is the outer shadow. People in the umbra see a total eclipse, and people in the penumbra see a partial eclipse.
  **12.** What happens during a partial lunar eclipse?
- **Answer:** The Earth's shadow covers only a section of the Moon. One section looks completely dark while the rest remains illuminated.
  **13.** Why does the Moon shine in the sky?
- **Answer:** The Moon has no light of its own. It shines by reflecting the light of the Sun.
  **14.** What would happen if the Moon were slightly smaller or farther away?
- **Answer:** Total solar eclipses would never happen, because the Moon would not completely cover the Sun.
  **15.** Which two types of shadows does the Moon cast on the Earth?
- **Answer:** The Moon

### Section F: Define the Following Terms

**1.** **Peninsula:** It is a landmass surrounded by water on three sides. The southern part of India is a peninsula.
**2.** **Subcontinent:** It is a large landmass that is a part of a bigger continent.
**3.** **Maritime:** It means bordering the sea.
**4.** **Civilisations:** They are developed and organised human societies.
**5.** **Arid:** It means dry.
**6.** **Archipelago:** It is a group or chain of islands. The Maldives is an archipelago of 1,192 coral islands.
**7.** **Indian subcontinent:** It is the region that includes India, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.
**8.** **Bioluminescence:** It is the magical blue shine seen on certain beaches of the Maldives at night. It happens because of tiny marine organisms called phytoplankton.
**9.** **Silk Route:** It was the major trade route that connected China with the rest of the world.
**10.** **Terai region:** It is the part of the Himalayas shared by India and Nepal. It was once rocky and unproductive but is now fertile farmland.

### Section G: Short Questions with Answers (2 Marks Each)

**1.** Name the waterbodies that surround India on three sides.

- **Answer:** The Arabian Sea in the west, the Indian Ocean in the south and the Bay of Bengal in the east.
  **2.** Why is the southern part of India called a peninsula?
- **Answer:** It has waterbodies on three sides: the Arabian Sea, the Indian Ocean and the Bay of Bengal.
  **3.** Why is India unique among countries?
- **Answer:** India is the only country in the world which has an ocean named after it, the Indian Ocean.
  **4.** What marks the northern boundary of India?
- **Answer:** The high snow-covered ranges of the Himalayas mark the northern boundary of India.
  **5.** Which countries are included in the Indian subcontinent?
- **Answer:** India, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka.
  **6.** Name the land and maritime neighbours of India.
- **Answer:** The land neighbours are Afghanistan, Pakistan, China, Bhutan, Nepal, Myanmar and Bangladesh. The maritime neighbours are Sri Lanka and Maldives.
  **7.** With which country does India share the longest and the shortest border?
- **Answer:** India shares the longest border with Bangladesh and the shortest with Afghanistan.
  **8.** Why is the Terai region important?
- **Answer:** The Terai region of the Himalayas is shared by India and Nepal. Though once rocky and unproductive, it is now fertile farmland.
  **9.** What are the main occupations in Nepal?
- **Answer:** Farming and cattle rearing are the main occupations. Nepal grows rice, wheat, maize and tea.
  **10.** Why is River Kosi called the "Sorrow of Bihar"?
- **Answer:** River Kosi has its source in Nepal and flows into India. The textbook notes it is known as the "Sorrow of Bihar" (it is known for floods).
  **11.** Why is Bhutan called the "Land of the Thunder Dragon"?
- **Answer:** It is a small Buddhist kingdom in the eastern Himalayas, and "Land of the Thunder Dragon" is its popular name.
  **12.** Why is Bhutan called the world's only carbon-negative country?
- **Answer:** Its forests cover about 70% of the land and absorb more carbon dioxide than the country produces.
  **13.** When did Bangladesh come into existence?
- **Answer:** In 1947, Bangladesh separated from India and became East Pakistan. It formally came into existence as Bangladesh in 1971 after breaking away from Pakistan.
  **14.** Name the main crops and industries of Bangladesh.
- **Answer:** The main crops are rice and jute. The major industries are textile, garment manufacturing and jute processing.
  **15.** What is the importance of the Indus Valley Civilisation in Pakistan?
- **Answer:** The ancient Indus Valley Civilisation flourished in the southern Indus valley, now in Pakistan. The important sites of Harappa and Mohenjo-daro are located in Pakistan.
  **16.** Name the tributaries of the River Indus.
- **Answer:** The tributaries of the Indus are the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi and Sutlej.
  **17.** Why is Sri Lanka called the "Pearl of the Indian Ocean"?
- **Answer:** It is called so because of its shape. It is an island country to the south-east of India.
  **18.** Which two waterbodies separate India from Sri Lanka?
- **Answer:** The Gulf of Mannar and the Palk Strait separate India from Sri Lanka.
  **19.** What is the major religion of Sri Lanka?
- **Answer:** Buddhism is the major religion, practised by almost 70% of the population.
  **20.** Why is the Maldives called an archipelago?
- **Answer:** It is a group of 1,192 coral islands, and only a few of them are inhabited.
  **21.** What is the most important industry of the Maldives?
- **Answer:** Tourism is the most important industry of the Maldives. Fishing and farming, with coconut as the main crop, are the main occupations.
  **22.** What are the historical evidences of India's relation with China?
- **Answer:** Ancient Indian books like the Manusmriti and the Mahabharata mention China. Hiuen Tsang, a Chinese Buddhist monk, came to India and wrote about its social and cultural structure.
  **23.** What is the Silk Route?
- **Answer:** It was the major trade route through which China was well connected with the rest of the world.
  **24.** Name the deserts and major rivers of China.
- **Answer:** The deserts are the Gobi and Taklamakan. The major rivers are the Yangtze and the Yellow River.
  **25.** Why was the name of Burma changed?
- **Answer:** Myanmar was earlier known as Burma. The name was changed to Myanmar in 1989.
  **26.** Why are the Irrawaddy and Salween rivers important to Myanmar?
- **Answer:** Their lower valley regions and fertile delta form one of the great rice granaries of the world.
  **27.** Name the two biggest cities of Myanmar.
- **Answer:** Yangon and Mandalay.
  **28.** What are the latitudinal and longitudinal extents of India?
- **Answer:** The latitudinal extent is 8° 4' N to 37° 6' N. The longitudinal extent is 68° 7' E to 97° 25' E.
  **29.** What are the north-south and east-west extents of India?
- **Answer:** The north-south extent is 3,214 km, and the east-west extent is 2,933 km.
  **30.** What is the length of India's land boundary and coastline?
- **Answer:** The land boundary is 15,200 km, and the coastline is 7,516 km.
