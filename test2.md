**1.** Where do you think the people in the poem are?

- **Answer:** They are most probably soldiers on a battlefield during World War I. They have a brief moment of relief from the war.

- **Answer:** He compares his delight to that of prisoned birds finding freedom. They fly wildly across white orchards and green fields until they are out of sight.

**3.** How did beauty come?

**4.** What did the poet feel when he heard the singing?

**10.** What does the line "Was a bird; and the song was wordless" mean?

**12.** Can you find a rhyming pattern in the poem?

### Section G: Short Answer Questions (2 Marks Each)

**1.** Define work with an example.

- **Answer:** Work is said to be done when a force applied on an object causes it to move a certain distance in the direction of the force. Example: pushing a cart through some distance.

**2.** Define energy.

- **Answer:** Energy is the capacity or ability to do work. Whenever work is done, there is always a change or transfer of energy.

**3.** Give an example of a situation where force is applied without doing work.

- **Answer:** Pushing a wall. We may get tired, but no work is done because the wall does not move.

**4.** Under what circumstances is work said to be done?

- **Answer:** Work is said to be done when the object on which the force is applied moves a certain distance in the direction of the force.

**5.** Differentiate between work and energy.

- **Answer:** Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance. Energy is the capacity to do work. Example: we use energy to throw a stone, and the act of throwing it is the work done.

**6.** Why is no work done when a person holds a heavy load without moving?

- **Answer:** The load does not move through a distance in the direction of the force, so no work is done.

**7.** Why do we say that the amount of energy needed depends on the task?

- **Answer:** Different tasks need different amounts of energy. For example, we need more energy to climb a staircase than to walk a few steps on plain ground.

**8.** What are energy-giving foods? Name five.

- **Answer:** Foods rich in carbohydrates and fats are called energy-giving foods. Examples: rice, wheat, potato, sugar, ghee.

**9.** Write the names of any five types of energy.

- **Answer:** Mechanical energy, heat energy, electrical energy, wind energy, sound energy.

**10.** What is mechanical energy? Give an example.

- **Answer:** The energy that an object possesses due to its position or movement is called mechanical energy. Example: water stored at a height behind a dam.

**11.** What is heat energy? Name two sources.

- **Answer:** Heat energy is released by burning fuels and is used for cooking and drying clothes. Sources: LPG, coal and the sun.

**12.** What is wind energy? How is it used?

- **Answer:** The energy present in moving air is called wind energy. Windmills convert it into electrical energy.

**13.** What is solar energy?

- **Answer:** The energy obtained from the rays of the sun is called solar energy. We also get light and heat energy from the sun.

**14.** How is sound energy produced? Give an example.

- **Answer:** Sound energy is produced when an object vibrates. Example: the bell of an alarm clock vibrates and we hear the alarm ring.

**15.** What is electrical energy?

- **Answer:** Electrical energy is generated at power stations and supplied to our homes. It runs appliances such as radios, televisions and computers.

**16.** Define renewable energy sources with examples.

- **Answer:** Energy sources that last for a long period of time are called renewable energy sources. Examples: sun, water and wind.

**17.** Define non-renewable energy sources with examples.

- **Answer:** Energy sources present in a limited amount that do not last long are called non-renewable energy sources. Examples: coal, petroleum and natural gas.

**18.** Why should we use non-renewable energy sources judiciously?

- **Answer:** They cannot be easily made by natural means, so we should use them wisely and carefully.

**19.** Which energy does a windmill use? Is it renewable or non-renewable?

- **Answer:** A windmill uses wind energy. It is renewable because wind lasts for a long period of time. Windmills generate electricity, which is beneficial for us.

**20.** What does the paper catching fire over a candle flame show?

- **Answer:** It shows that heat is a form of energy, as the candle flame contains heat energy.

**21.** What does SDG 7 aim to ensure?

- **Answer:** SDG 7 aims to ensure access to affordable, reliable, sustainable and modern energy for all.

### Section G: Short Answer Questions (2 Marks Each)

**1.** What is a machine?

- **Answer:** A machine is a tool that makes our work easier. It helps us overcome a larger force by applying a lesser force called effort.

**2.** Why do we use machines?

- **Answer:** There are many tasks we cannot do ourselves, or they need a lot of time and effort. Machines make such work easier.

**3.** What is a simple machine? Give one example.

- **Answer:** A simple machine uses a single applied force to get the work done and has very few parts. Example: a wheelbarrow.

**4.** What is a complex machine?

- **Answer:** A complex machine consists of two or more simple machines.

**5.** What is a lever? Name the three types of levers.

- **Answer:** A lever is a rigid bar resting on a pivot. It is used to move a heavy load with one end when pressure is applied on the other end. The three types are class-one, class-two and class-three levers.

**6.** Define fulcrum, load and effort.

- **Answer:** Fulcrum is the fixed point around which the rod moves. Load is the object on which work is to be done. Effort is the external force applied for the rod to do its work.

**7.** What is a class-one lever? Give two examples.

- **Answer:** A lever in which the fulcrum is located between the load and the effort. Examples: scissors and seesaw.

**8.** What is a class-two lever? Give two examples.

- **Answer:** A lever in which the load is located between the fulcrum and the effort. Examples: wheelbarrow and nutcracker.

**9.** What is a class-three lever? Give two examples.

- **Answer:** A lever in which the effort is located between the fulcrum and the load. Examples: fishing rod and tweezers.

**10.** Why is a pair of scissors a class-one lever?

- **Answer:** The fulcrum lies between the load (paper) and the effort, where the two parts of the scissors are joined.

**11.** Why is a nutcracker a class-two lever?

- **Answer:** The effort is applied at one end and the fulcrum is at the other end, while the load (walnut) lies between the effort and the fulcrum.

**12.** Why is a pair of tongs a class-three lever?

- **Answer:** The fulcrum is at one end and the load is at the other end, while the effort is applied in the middle, between the fulcrum and the load.

**13.** What is a pulley?

- **Answer:** A pulley is a simple machine used to lift heavy objects. It has a wheel with a grooved rim and a rope, chain or belt wound around it, and the wheel rotates around an axle.

**14.** What is a fixed pulley? Give an example.

- **Answer:** A fixed pulley has a fixed axle and makes work easier by changing the direction of the force. Example: drawing a bucket of water from a well.

**15.** What is a movable pulley? Give an example.

- **Answer:** A movable pulley has a free or movable axle and is used along with a fixed pulley. Example: a crane.

**16.** Rohan finds it difficult to pull a bucket of water up from a well. How can a pulley help him?

- **Answer:** A fixed pulley changes the direction of the force, so Rohan can pull the rope downward to lift the bucket. This makes the work easier.

**17.** What is a wheel and axle? Give two examples.

- **Answer:** It is a simple machine made up of a wheel rigidly attached to a rod called an axle. Examples: steering wheel and doorknob.

**18.** What is an inclined plane? Give two examples.

- **Answer:** An inclined plane is a tilted surface or slope used to move a heavy load with less effort. Examples: ramps in hospitals and slides on the playground.

**19.** What is a screw? Give its use.

- **Answer:** A screw is a modified inclined plane with a groove at its head where a screwdriver tip fits. It is used to hold things tightly together.

**20.** What are screw jacks?

- **Answer:** Screw jacks are big screws used to lift cars and other heavy objects such as logs of wood and iron plates.

**21.** Define wedge.

- **Answer:** A wedge is a simple machine with a long, triangular shape, with one broad or blunt end and one sharp end. The sharp end is used to cut or slice objects.

**22.** Why can a knife cut an apple but a pencil cannot?

- **Answer:** The edge of a knife acts as a wedge with a sharp end that easily slices the apple. A pencil has no such sharp edge.

**23.** Name the six simple machines.

- **Answer:** Lever, pulley, wheel and axle, wedge, screw and inclined plane.

**24.** Give two examples of wedges.

- **Answer:** The head of an axe and the blade of a knife.

### Section F: Define the Following Terms

**1.** **Eclipse:** An eclipse is an astronomical event that takes place when one heavenly body, such as the Moon or a planet, moves into the shadow of another heavenly body.

**2.** **Solar eclipse:** It occurs when the Moon comes between the Sun and the Earth and blocks the light of the Sun from reaching the Earth.

**3.** **Lunar eclipse:** It occurs on a full moon night when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon, and only the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.

**4.** **Umbra:** It is the dark central part of the shadow cast by the Moon on the Earth.

**5.** **Penumbra:** It is the outer shadow that gets bigger while reaching the Earth.

**6.** **Total solar eclipse:** It occurs when the umbra covers a small part of the Earth and the Moon completely covers the Sun. It can be seen only from the exact location.

**7.** **Partial solar eclipse:** It is the eclipse seen by people in the penumbra location, where the Moon covers only a part of the Sun.

**8.** **Total lunar eclipse:** It occurs when the Moon and the Sun are on exact opposite sides of the Earth and the Moon appears red.

**9.** **Partial lunar eclipse:** It occurs when the Earth's shadow covers only a section of the Moon. One section looks completely dark while the rest remains illuminated.

**10.** **Orbiting:** It means to move along a curved path.

**11.** **Celestial bodies:** They are heavenly bodies, such as the Sun, Moon, Earth and planets, that are part of the universe.
